01Fantasy & Tone

You are a travelling bovine podiatrist. A big, patient cow is lifted gently in the chute, one caked hoof presented to you. Your job is to read what's wrong under the mud and the overgrown horn, choose the right tool, and bring the foot back to a flat, balanced, weight-bearing surface — so the animal walks off sound.

The emotional target is the exact feeling people describe watching Nate the Hoof Guy (Nate Ranallo, a one-man hoof-care business in West Salem, Wisconsin, with 1M+ followers on each of YouTube, TikTok and Facebook): calm, methodical, informative, and quietly triumphant. The satisfaction comes from four real beats his viewers call out — the mud-caked "before," the careful reveal of the hidden problem, the clean pass that releases trapped pressure, and the cow walking away comfortable. Nate himself describes the appeal as "the instant gratification of fixing a problem." boingboing / Outside / Hoard's

The anti-cheese principle

This is deliberately not a pimple-popping game. Pop games reward gross-out spectacle and mindless tapping. TRUE HOOF rewards judgment: knowing that the dark spot is a white-line lesion and not an ulcer, that you lower the affected claw and block the sound one, that you never chase a model into the toe triangle. The payoff is craft — "I understood this foot and fixed it correctly" — not shock. Respect for the animal is a design constraint, not a disclaimer: trimming is framed, truthfully, as painless when done right ("akin to clipping a toenail"), and drawing blood is always a failure, never a reward. Outside / AHDB

02Design Pillars

Read before you cut

Every hoof is a small diagnostic puzzle. The player wins by seeing correctly — overgrowth vs. lesion vs. foreign body — then sequencing tools, not by speed.

Tactile, not twitchy

Gestures map to real motions: swipe to pare, scrub to clean, drag to measure. It should feel like working a hoof in your hand.

Balance is the win state

Correctness & even weight-bearing score, not clear-the-screen tapping. You can "finish" a hoof and still have done it wrong.

Do no harm

The sensitive corium is a hard boundary. Cut into the quick and the cow flinches, it bleeds, the case fails. Caution is always rewarded over aggression.

03Core Gameplay Loop

One case = one hoof (sometimes a whole foot = two claws). The loop is designed so the player is thinking about tool choice and order at every beat, mirroring how a real trimmer approaches an unknown foot.

INTAKERead the hoofCow enters the chute, foot lifts. The hoof is dirty, overgrown, with hidden problems. A short "assessment" phase lets you look — nothing is labelled for you yet.
CLEANScrub & dry to revealWire-brush/hoof-knife off mud and loose matter. This is the first "reveal": clues (black marks, cracks, soft spots) appear only once clean. Real protocol opens with "clean and dry the foot." AHDB · pre-trim
DIAGNOSETap suspect zonesUse the hoof testers / probe to confirm what you think you see. Pressure on a lesion "springs" or shows a soft spot — turning a guess into a committed diagnosis. Wrong-tool-wrong-place wastes time but never harms.
TRIMWork the five steps in orderThe heart of the game — functional trimming, then therapy only if the foot needs it (see §4). Tool choice and sequence are the puzzle.
TREATRelieve & finishWhere there's a lesion: dish out weight, block the sound claw, remove loose horn, expose infected tissue to air.
RESOLVEThe walk-offScoring resolves, then the reward shot: the cow is lowered and walks away level. Comfortable gait = the real payoff.

Average case length: 60–120 seconds — short enough for a phone session, long enough to feel earned. Players chain 3–5 cases per "farm visit."

04The Five-Step Backbone

The trimming core is a direct, legible adaptation of the five-step Dutch method — steps 1–3 are functional (done on every foot), steps 4–5 are therapeutic (only when there's a lesion). This real structure is what makes the game a sequencing puzzle instead of a scrub-fest. AHDB five-step method = attached five-step protocol

StepReal protocolIn-game mechanic
1 · Measure & trim first clawOn a hind foot start with the inner claw; measure toe from where horn goes hard (~1 cm below hairline) to ~8–8.5 cm; pare excess sole to a ~6–8 mm toe step; don't touch the heel.A "measure" overlay drags down the toe; player trims to a target band. Over-shortening thins the sole and raises a lameness-risk flag — the game literally tells you thin sole = risk. AHDB Step 1
2 · Match opposing clawLevel the outer claw up to the first; usually more horn off the outer, minimal off the inner, keep weight-bearing. Don't cut the axial wall/white line.A balance meter shows the two claws' relative height. Player pares the taller claw to level them. A protected "toe triangle" zone cannot be cut without penalty. AHDB Step 2
3 · Dish out the clawsModel a "dish" behind the inner wall edges so muck flows between the toes and weight comes off the typical sole-ulcer site — without extending into the toe triangle.A guided swipe carves the dish along a highlighted channel. Going too deep or too far forward (into the triangle) removes weight-bearing wall = penalty. This is the signature "skill" gesture. AHDB Step 3
4 · Relieve the affected clawCreate a height difference for the painful claw; apply a block to the sound partner claw so the injured one bears less weight and can heal.Only unlocked when a lesion is diagnosed. Player lowers the affected claw and glues a block to the correct (sound) claw — blocking the wrong claw is a classic failure. AHDB Step 4
5 · Remove loose hornRemove loose/undermined horn from the heel; check between claws & at the heel for digital dermatitis; don't strip too much weight-bearing heel.Spreading-pliers gesture opens the claws to inspect; knife flicks away only horn flagged as detached. Removing attached, healthy horn costs points. AHDB Step 5
Golden rule carried from the manuals into every scoring pass: "Avoid over-trimming — always err on the side of caution." As sole thickness decreases, lameness risk rises. The game should consistently reward the restrained, correct cut over the dramatic one. AHDB

05Tool Set

Each tool does one job well and the wrong job badly — so picking the tool is the decision. Players start with three and unlock the rest through the career.

🖌️
Wire brush / wash scrub gesture
Removes mud and surface muck to reveal the hoof. Can't cut horn. The mandatory opening move — you cannot diagnose a dirty foot.
🔪
Hoof knife short precise swipe
The scalpel. Thin paring, opening a lesion, releasing trapped pressure, flicking off loose horn. High control, low volume. Held "behind the blade" — a safety detail the game can teach. tool review / AHDB kit
✂️
Nippers pinch / tap to bite
Takes off bulk overgrown toe in clean bites. Fast horn removal but blunt — useless for fine finishing and dangerous near lesions. Quality nippers > "clumsy choppers." AHDB kit
⚙️
Angle grinder + trimming disc sustained glide, pressure-sensitive
The power tool. Levels and models large surfaces fast; grit tiers (coarse→fine) unlock for bulk vs. finishing. Powerful and risky — overrun into the corium and you draw blood. Real evidence: a proper disc can improve quality and lower hazard. tool review
🪵
Rasp back-and-forth stroke
Finishing and smoothing; coarse side for quick take-down, fine side to true the surface. The "make it clean" tool that earns finish points.
🧪
Hoof testers / probe tap to apply pressure
Diagnostic, not cutting. Confirms where it hurts — springs a loose sole, finds the soft spot over an abscess. Converts a visual guess into a committed diagnosis.
🧱
Block + glue place & press-hold
Therapeutic. Raises the sound claw so the injured one heals. Placing it on the wrong claw is a headline mistake. AHDB Step 4
💨
Spray / topical + air tap & swipe to dry
For digital dermatitis and exposed lesions — expose to air, apply topical. Finishing care that closes infected cases. AHDB / pathology
Secondary kit referenced for flavour/realism in later tiers: measuring gauge for toe length & sole thickness, spreading pliers (to open the claws), gloves/face-shield/gown (presented as unlockable "pro rig" cosmetics that nod to the real danger of the job). AHDB kit / tool review

06Touch Controls

Everything is one-thumb friendly and gesture-mapped to the physical motion. No twitch timing; all gestures reward smoothness and control over speed.

Tap

Select a tool from the radial dock; tap a zone with the probe to test it.

Swipe / pare

Short directional swipes with the knife remove thin horn along the swipe path. Depth scales gently with swipe length — long reckless swipes risk the corium.

Scrub

Rapid back-and-forth to clean mud or rasp a surface smooth. Satisfying friction haptic.

Press & hold (pressure)

Grinder and nippers: hold to keep removing material; a subtle resistance/haptic builds as you near the safe limit — let go in time.

Drag guides

Measuring overlay and the "dish" channel are drawn by dragging along an on-hoof guide line — keeps the skill legible on a small screen.

Two-finger rotate (optional)

Rotate the hoof to inspect the heel, interdigital space, and axial wall. Kept optional so the game is fully playable one-handed.

Assist & accessibility: a "guided lines" toggle (faint target bands & no-cut zones shown) for new players, dialled back on harder tiers; haptics and sound both independently toggleable; colour-blind-safe lesion markers (shape + colour).

07Anatomy of a Case (start to finish)

Here is a single hoof — a hind foot with an outer-claw sole ulcer — played end to end, to show how the beats, tools and the five steps interlock.

0:00 · PRESENTThe caked "before"Hind foot lifts, lateral claw visibly overgrown, everything brown with slurry. Chute creak, the cow settles. Player sees a mess and a hint of a dark patch.
0:08 · CLEANScrub revealWire-brush gesture clears mud. A darker, bruised zone emerges on the sole of the outer claw near the typical ulcer site. First dopamine hit: order from chaos.
0:20 · DIAGNOSECommit the readProbe taps the dark zone — it "springs," confirming a sole ulcer (not just bruising). The case objective locks: functional trim + relieve the affected claw.
0:30 · STEP 1–2Measure & balanceNippers take the bulk overgrowth off the toe; measure overlay confirms ~8 cm; match the claws level, protecting the toe triangle. Balance meter swings toward centre.
0:55 · STEP 3The dishKnife/grinder carves the guided dish behind the inner wall, pulling weight off the ulcer site. Staying out of the triangle earns the "clean model" bonus.
1:10 · STEP 4Relieve & blockLower the affected outer claw slightly; glue a block onto the sound inner claw. Get the claw right → satisfying "clunk" + green confirm.
1:25 · STEP 5FinishSpreading pliers open the claws, knife flicks the one flagged curl of loose heel horn, quick DD check between the bulbs comes back clean. Rasp trues the surface.
1:35 · RESOLVEThe walk-offScore tallies (balance, correctness, restraint, finish). Cow lowers, takes a few level steps — no limp. "Sound." Shavings swept; next cow.

08Scoring — Balance & Correctness, not Speed

Scoring exists to reinforce craft. Speed is never a primary score. A hoof is graded on how right it is, how little harm was done, and how clean the finish looks.

ComponentWeightWhat it measures
Balance35%Even weight-bearing surface, claws level, correct toe length/angle, heel preserved. The core of functional trimming. AHDB 1–3
Correctness30%Right diagnosis, right tools in the right order, block on the sound claw, lesion properly relieved/exposed. AHDB 4–5
Restraint20%Minimal over-trimming; sole kept thick enough; healthy/attached horn left alone. Rewards caution per the golden rule.
Finish15%Smooth, true surface, muck cleared, loose horn gone — the aesthetic "clean hoof" payoff.

Fail / penalty states (harm > haste)

  • Drawing blood — cutting into the corium/quick. The cow flinches, red appears, the case drops a grade or fails. The single hardest line in the game. AHDB / Outside
  • Over-thinning the sole — raises explicit lameness-risk; tanks Restraint & Balance.
  • Cutting the toe triangle / axial wall — removes weight-bearing horn; Balance penalty.
  • Blocking the wrong (affected) claw — defeats the therapy; Correctness penalty.
  • Removing attached healthy horn — Restraint penalty.

Grading uses a trimmer's-ticket feel: Sound → Clean → Textbook (bronze/silver/gold), plus a herd "mobility score" that trends up across a farm visit when you trim well.

09Feedback & Juice — the satisfaction engine

The game lives or dies on how the reveal and the clean pass feel. Every satisfying beat Nate's audience names has a direct sensory hook here.

The reveal

Mud wipes away in wet, clumping strokes; the clean hoof underneath is a visible before/after. Problems only surface once clean — earning the "oh, that's what's wrong" moment. why-satisfying research

The clean pass

Horn shavings peel and curl off the blade ("forbidden parmesan"), each pass with a crisp shk sound and a short haptic tick. Long smooth passes feel best — reinforcing control.

Pressure release

When a lesion/abscess is opened correctly, trapped fluid escapes with a soft release cue (sound + tiny particle puff + relief haptic). This is the catharsis beat — handled cleanly, never gratuitously.

The walk-off

The emotional close: cow lowers and strides away level. A gentle success chime, a calm "Sound." stamp, the mobility score ticking up. Nate's "instant gratification" in one shot.

Audio / haptics

ASMR-leaning throughout: ambient barn room-tone, soft chute creaks, the friction of brush and rasp, the clean peel of the blade. Optional calm, Wisconsin-ish narrator voice explaining the foot — echoing why Nate's narration is called "calming and informative." Distinct haptic textures per tool (scrub = fine rumble, blade = sharp tick, grinder = sustained buzz that tightens near the danger limit). All subtle, never arcade-loud.

10Progression & Meta — Apprentice to Pro

A career arc gives the puzzle room to deepen without ever becoming a speed grind.

TierUnlocksComplexity
ApprenticeBrush, hoof knife, nippers. Guided lines on. Routine overgrowth only.Steps 1–3, single claw, generous target bands.
TrimmerGrinder + coarse disc, rasp, hoof testers. First lesions appear.Full foot (two claws), diagnosis matters, tighter bands.
SpecialistBlocks, spray/topical, fine discs, measuring gauge, spreading pliers.Therapeutic steps 4–5, multi-problem feet, guided lines dialled back.
Pro / Road Trimmer"Pro rig" cosmetics, chute upgrades, new animals, timed exhibition modes (optional).Rare/compound pathologies, minimal assists, full judgment.

Variety axes

Cases: a growing deck of pathologies (overgrowth, sole ulcer, white-line lesion/abscess, toe abscess, embedded foreign body, hoof crack/fissure, digital dermatitis, heel-horn erosion, corkscrew claw). Animals: dairy cows first, then beef cattle, the "occasional goat" (a real Nate detail), and farther out, horses/sheep as distinct trimming rulesets. Settings: home farm, visiting herds, a county-fair exhibition, a "worst feet you've ever seen" rescue set. A light farm-management meta (build your route, upgrade the chute/truck, raise herd mobility scores) gives long-term goals without pressuring the per-hoof pace.

11Example Cases (drawn from real pathologies)

CASE A — "The Overgrown Toe" · Apprentice

Simple functional trim

A dairy cow overdue for maintenance: long, curled toes, no disease. Objective: steps 1–3 only. Measure the inner hind claw to ~8 cm, match the outer, dish both — protecting the toe triangle and the heel. Teaches: the measure→match→dish rhythm and the golden rule of restraint. Fail lines: over-shortening the sole, cutting the heel. AHDB Steps 1–3

CASE B — "Pressure Point" · Trimmer

Sole ulcer, lateral hind claw

The classic. Dark bruised zone at the typical ulcer site on the outer hind claw. Flow: clean → probe to confirm it springs → functional trim → dish to pull weight off the ulcer → lower the affected claw and block the sound inner claw → remove loose horn. Fail lines: blocking the wrong claw; leaving weight on the lesion. AHDB Step 4 / pathology: ulcers common in lateral hind claw, treat by weight relief + block on healthy claw

CASE C — "The Hidden Screw" · Trimmer

Embedded foreign body

Straight from one of Nate's most-watched videos: a blackened puncture on the sole where a screw/stone is lodged. Flow: clean to expose the dark entry point → probe to locate → carefully pare down and extract the object → open the tract so trapped pressure escapes (the catharsis beat) → relieve & protect. Fail lines: gouging past the object into the corium. Nate screw video / pathology

CASE D — "White Line" · Specialist

White-line lesion → toe/white-line abscess

Separation along the white line packed with dark debris, tracking to an abscess. Flow: clean → trace the white line → trim the adjacent wall and white-line horn to release the pressure, trimming the wall back to the depth removed so no crater traps muck → block the sound claw for larger cavities → expose to air. Note (realism): no antibiotics for a simple white-line abscess; deep extension would be a vet referral — a good "know your limits" beat. Fail lines: leaving a crater; cutting live tissue. pathology: white-line/toe abscess

CASE E — "Between the Heels" · Specialist

Digital dermatitis

Painful raw lesion at the back of the hind foot, between the heel bulbs. Flow: clean gently → spreading pliers to inspect the interdigital space → identify DD (not a horn lesion) → expose to air + apply topical; trimming is minimal here — the skill is recognising that this is a skin disease, not something to carve. Fail lines: over-trimming healthy horn chasing a skin problem. AHDB Step 5 / pathology: DD at heel bulbs

CASE F — "The Crack" · Pro

Vertical hoof-wall fissure

A sand-crack up the wall. Flow: clean → assess depth with the probe → stabilise: relieve horn around the crack so it bears no leverage, remove undermined horn, smooth the edges → block if the claw is painful. Fail lines: aggressive grinding that weakens the wall or reaches sensitive tissue. pathology: horn disorders — remove detached horn, avoid live tissue

12Monetization

The calm, craft tone should steer away from aggressive, interrupt-driven ads — those would wreck the ASMR mood the whole product is selling. Recommended, in order of fit:

★ Premium / one-time unlock (best fit)

A paid "Pro Trimmer" upgrade (or paid app) removes limits and ads entirely. The relaxing, premium feel supports a clean paid product — closest to the "oddly satisfying, respectful" brand.

Rewarded video (opt-in only)

Player chooses to watch an ad for a cosmetic, an extra case, or a hint. Never interstitials mid-trim — the mood is the product.

Cosmetic IAP

Tool skins, custom chutes/trucks, barn themes, breed packs, narrator voice packs. No pay-to-win: skill is judgment, which can't be bought.

Content / case packs (DLC)

Themed case decks ("Rescue Herd," "County Fair," goats, horses), a light energy-free battle-pass of weekly cases. Expansion, not gating.
Explicitly avoid: energy/lives timers, forced interstitials, and gross-out "shock" ad creatives — all three fight the product's identity. A gentle daily "farm visit" streak is enough retention hook.

13Art & Audio Direction

Visual

Warm, grounded, semi-realistic — closer to a tactile craft game than a cartoon. Earthy barn palette, soft directional light on the hoof as the hero object. The hoof itself is the high-detail star: layered horn, visible white line, believable mud and shavings, honest-but-not-gory lesions. Readable at phone size — clear silhouettes, shape-coded problem markers. Restrained, never lurid; the "ick" is handled with the same matter-of-fact calm Nate brings.

Audio

ASMR-first. Low barn room-tone, chute creaks, brush friction, the clean peel of horn off the blade, grinder hum. A calm, informative optional narrator (a warm Midwestern register, consciously evoking Nate's "calming and informative" delivery) that teaches as you go and can be muted. Success is understated — a soft "Sound." and a gentle chime, never a slot-machine jingle. Music is sparse, ambient, low-tempo; the work sounds are the soundtrack.

14MVP Scope

Goal of the MVP: prove the core feeling — read → choose tool → clean pass → sound walk-off — and the anti-cheese "balance over tapping" hook, with the smallest content set that tests it.

In scope (v0.1)

Dairy cow, hind foot only 4 tools: brush, knife, nippers, probe Grinder + rasp (1 disc) Five-step trim, functional 1–3 Therapy 4–5 (block + loose horn) 3 pathologies: overgrowth, sole ulcer, foreign body Clean/reveal + walk-off juice Balance/Correctness/Restraint/Finish scoring Blood = fail state ~15 hand-built cases + guided tutorial Core haptics + ASMR sound pass

Out of scope (later)

Front feet & other animals Full pathology deck (white line, DD, crack…) Farm-management meta & map Narrator voice packs Cosmetics / IAP store Procedural case generation Exhibition / timed modes

Build order: (1) one hoof, one tool, the clean-pass gesture + shavings + sound — find the feel. (2) Add the five-step sequence & balance meter — find the puzzle. (3) Add diagnosis + a lesion case + the block — find the depth. (4) Juice pass: reveal, pressure-release, walk-off. (5) Tutorial + 15-case tuning. Ship to a small test group and measure whether players feel craft, not grind.

15Assumptions, Decisions & Sources

Key decisions I made

  • Format: delivered as a self-contained HTML design doc (looks better than raw markdown and opens anywhere). Markdown export is trivial if you prefer.
  • Five-step protocol as the spine. I committed to mapping the trimming core 1:1 onto the Dutch five-step method — it's what turns the game into a sequencing puzzle instead of a scrubber, exactly as you asked.
  • Working title "TRUE HOOF" — a placeholder chosen to signal craft/realism; swap freely.
  • Premium-first monetization. Given the calm/ASMR identity, I prioritised a paid unlock + opt-in rewarded ads over interstitials, which would damage the mood.
  • Dairy-cow/hind-foot MVP. Narrowed the first build to the single most iconic, most content-rich starting point (hind feet carry the classic lesions).
  • Blood as the hard fail line — the clearest expression of "respect the animal, reward caution."

Assumptions

  • Target platform: iOS/Android, portrait, one-handed play; casual-session length (1–2 min per case).
  • Audience: the broad "oddly satisfying" crowd Nate reaches, not just farmers — so realism is the texture, with assists keeping it accessible.
  • No licensing deal with Nate Ranallo assumed; he's cited as inspiration only. (A real collaboration/endorsement would be a strong growth lever if pursued — worth a conversation.)
  • Specific numbers (8–8.5 cm toe, 6–8 mm sole step) are design targets drawn from AHDB; tune for feel, and reconcile against your attached manuals once reachable.
